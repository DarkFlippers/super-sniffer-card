# Changelog

## 0.1

- Initial release: read the authentications a Gen2 Super Sniffer card captured from a reader, and append them to the MFKey nonce log so MFKey can recover the keys
- Read the card's trace slots with plain `30 00`..`30 07`, the same commands the vendor's Android app uses on its read path, and judge each slot on its contents rather than its index - the command byte has to be one a reader would send, and a slot that captured nothing leaves the reader's two words zero. The vendor app reads eight slots and Proxmark3 reads seven, and on the card the vendor ships test data for, the eighth holds the card's own UID followed by twelve zero bytes, so neither count is wrong and neither is a reason to truncate a card that does use eight
- Expand the stored tag nonce before writing it out. The card keeps `nt` as the 16-bit Crypto1 PRNG state rather than the nonce the reader saw, which loses nothing - the generator is a 16-bit LFSR - but has to be run forward 31 steps to get the nonce back. Verified against the vendor app's own test vector: its two sample records, parsed at these offsets and expanded this way, recover `A0A1A2A3A4A5` through Proxmark3's `mfkey32v2`
- Pair captures by sector and key type, not by block. One key covers every block in its sector and a reader walks a sector authenticating against whichever block it likes, so pairing on the block throws away most usable pairs
- Within the captures of one key, pair greedily and disjointly rather than writing every combination. A key falls out of any one valid pair, and each line costs MFKey minutes, so eight captures of one sector would otherwise turn into twenty-eight lines for one key
- Skip a pair whose two captures share a tag nonce. They carry the same keystream position, so mfkey32 has nothing to intersect - the line would cost MFKey its minutes and could not succeed
- Append to `/ext/nfc/.mfkey32.log` in the firmware's own line format, byte for byte, so nonces sniffed off a reader with the Flipper and nonces lifted off a sniffer card accumulate in the same place. The format matters exactly: MFKey does not parse this with a format string, it walks space-separated tokens and keeps the 5th, 7th, 9th, 11th, 13th, 15th and 17th
- Identify the card passively. It is recognised by answering an unauthenticated `30 00` while presenting a MIFARE Classic SAK - a genuine Classic refuses that read, and a plain Ultralight answers it but cannot present that SAK. The vendor app has a stronger signature, `30 F3` answering with `0x11`, but that byte is also the first step of the five-command sequence that arms a UID write, and nothing establishes what a card does with an unfinished knock. A read must not be able to change the card
- Say what a read found, per capture, with the sector, the key type and the block the reader asked for, and count separately the captures that have no second authentication of the same key - that is the one failure another tap on the reader fixes
